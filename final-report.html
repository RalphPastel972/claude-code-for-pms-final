Release 4.2 and the four responders who went quiet

On 12 August, Release 4.2 gave responders 60 seconds instead of 90 to answer a ping. A ping that runs out counts as a “no”, so far more responders slid down the queue. Four of them almost stopped being asked, and nothing told their handlers.

Part 1What happened

What happened, in plain words

On 12 August, Release 4.2 cut the time a responder has to answer a ping from 90 seconds to 60.

Dispatch counts a ping that runs out the same as a “no”, and every “no” moves a responder further back in the queue. With 30 seconds less, far more pings ran out.

Four responders, Vesper, Farlight, The Undertow and Meteor Mite, missed more than half their pings. They sank to the back of the queue and almost stopped being asked. Their neighbours picked up the work, and callouts took longer to fill.

Nothing in Dispatch lets a score recover on its own, and nothing warned their handlers.

And round again. Fewer pings means fewer chances to take a job, so the score stays down. Time doesn’t restore it, and nothing a handler does changes it.

Nobody is told

The console shows the handler that the responder is available, and nothing about why the phone has gone quiet.

The other change stays

4.2 also made distance count more than track record (0.45 → 0.60 against 0.40 → 0.25). Wide-area responders asked for that part.

Why these four missed so much more. The database has no answer times. Aunt Dot, Vesper’s handler, says Vesper loses the race down the stairs to the phone.

Part 2 · The evidencePing data

Missed pings rose for all 16 responders. Four were hit far harder.

Finding

From 12 August every responder missed more. For twelve, misses rose to 10–18%. For the four, more than half their pings ran out, and then the pings stopped.

They got about 12 pings a week each before 4.2, and 0 or 1 in the last week. The average hides this: the share of pings taken looked like it was recovering (73% in the last week), mostly because those four dropped out of it.

In plain English

  • −0.12A “no” and a ping that runs out cost the same. The code says so: “Same either way”.
  • +0.08Taking a job is the only thing that adds points.
  • 0Nothing else moves the score: not time, not a reset, not a handler. Wen Li’s 2019 note asks whether scores should recover on their own. It was never decided.

What we’d build

  • Kip sees a “gone quiet” warning with the reason in plain words.
  • Kip can put the responder back in the queue (new: today nothing a handler does changes their place). It restores their place once, and records that Kip did it.

Stating the problem would have shown there wasn’t one

Before 4.2, no ticket complained about the 90-second wait, and only 2.3% of pings ran out. At best, a 60-second wait saved under one second per callout. Nobody has found a written reason for 60.
